# Review Question

What stimulation parameters, mechanistic measures, and behavioral or clinical outcomes have been used in human studies of binaural auditory beat stimulation, and what evidence exists for translation into motor-control, autonomic regulation, and rehabilitation research?

## Subquestions

1. What beat frequencies, carrier frequencies, exposure durations, and delivery methods have been used?

2. 2. What control conditions have been used?
  
   3. 3. Do studies measure entrainment directly, or do they assume it?
     
      4. 4. Which neurophysiological measures are used, including EEG, MEG, ASSR, FFR, spectral power, connectivity, or autonomic markers?
        
         5. 5. Which behavioral and clinical outcomes are studied, including cognition, anxiety, mood, pain, sleep, motor performance, motor learning, tremor, rehabilitation, and HRV?
           
            6. 6. Which findings are most relevant to future work on sonification of a sine-wave visual motor template?
              
               7. ## PICO-S Framework
              
               8. **Population**: Human participants of any age, health status, or neurological condition.
              
               9. **Intervention**: Binaural auditory beat stimulation, binaural acoustic stimulation, binaural beat stimulation, or closely related beat-based auditory stimulation.
              
               10. **Comparator**: Silence, no stimulation, sham, pure tone, white noise, pink noise, music, monaural beats, alternative frequency conditions, or conventional acoustic stimulation.
              
               11. **Outcome**: Any neurophysiological, cognitive, affective, pain, sleep, motor, autonomic, or clinical outcome.
              
               12. **Study design**: Any empirical human study (RCT, crossover, quasi-experimental, observational).
              
               13. ## Notes on Scope
              
               14. This is a scoping review. The question is intentionally broad to map the full extent of the evidence. The motor-control and rehabilitation subquestion will receive particular attention to inform thesis development.

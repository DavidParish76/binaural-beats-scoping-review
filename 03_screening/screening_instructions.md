# screening_instructions

## Project

**Binaural Beats, Brainwave Entrainment, and Motor Control: A Scoping Review and Translational Framework**

## Screening Stage

This document governs title/abstract screening and full-text screening for the scoping review.

The purpose of screening is to create a defensible corpus of empirical human studies on binaural auditory beat stimulation and related auditory beat interventions, with special attention to mechanistic measures, behavioral/clinical outcomes, motor-control relevance, autonomic regulation, and rehabilitation translation.

---

## 1. Primary Review Question

What stimulation parameters, mechanistic measures, and behavioral or clinical outcomes have been used in human studies of binaural auditory beat stimulation, and what evidence exists for translation into motor-control, autonomic regulation, and rehabilitation research?

---

## 2. Screening Principle

Use a conservative inclusion approach during title/abstract screening.

If the title or abstract suggests possible relevance but does not provide enough information to decide, mark the record as:

```text
Maybe
```

Do not exclude a record merely because the abstract does not fully describe the stimulation parameters. Parameter adequacy can be judged during full-text screening.

---

## 3. Screening Decisions

Use only the following primary screening decisions.

| Decision | Meaning | Next Step |
|---|---|---|
| Include | Clearly eligible or very likely eligible | Retrieve full text |
| Maybe | Possibly eligible; insufficient information in title/abstract | Retrieve full text |
| Exclude | Clearly not eligible | Record exclusion reason |
| Background | Review, theoretical, methodological, or contextual source not eligible for primary extraction | Retain for background/citation chasing |

---

## 4. Inclusion Criteria

### 4.1 Population

Include studies involving human participants of any age group.

Eligible populations include:

- healthy adults
- children or adolescents
- older adults
- neurological populations
- psychiatric or anxiety-related populations
- pain-related populations
- rehabilitation populations
- Parkinson’s disease, stroke, or other motor-relevant clinical groups

### 4.2 Intervention

Include studies using at least one of the following:

- binaural beats
- binaural auditory beats
- binaural auditory beat stimulation
- binaural acoustic stimulation
- binaural beat stimulation
- auditory beat stimulation when binaural stimulation is clearly included
- brainwave entrainment interventions when binaural beats are separable as a component

### 4.3 Comparators

Eligible comparator conditions include:

- silence
- no stimulation
- sham stimulation
- pure tone
- carrier-tone-only condition
- white noise
- pink noise
- music
- monaural beats
- isochronic tones
- conventional acoustic stimulation
- alternative binaural beat frequency conditions

### 4.4 Outcomes

Include studies reporting at least one of the following outcome types:

- EEG, MEG, ASSR, FFR, ERP, spectral power, connectivity, or phase synchronization
- entrainment-related outcomes
- cognition, memory, attention, vigilance, executive function
- anxiety, mood, stress, relaxation, meditation-related states
- pain perception or clinical pain
- sleep or sleep-adjacent outcomes
- motor learning, motor performance, tremor, reaction time, error, dexterity, kinematics
- rehabilitation outcomes
- HRV, heart rate, blood pressure, autonomic regulation, physiological arousal

### 4.5 Study Designs

Include:

- randomized controlled trials
- crossover trials
- within-subject experimental studies
- between-group experimental studies
- quasi-experimental studies
- observational intervention studies
- pilot studies, if empirical data are reported

---

## 5. Exclusion Criteria

Exclude records when any of the following are clearly true:

1. The study does not involve human participants.
2. The paper is purely theoretical and contains no empirical human data.
3. The source is a commercial wellness report, product claim, blog post, or promotional material.
4. The study uses music, meditation, sound therapy, or auditory stimulation without a clearly identifiable binaural beat or auditory beat component.
5. The study uses a bundled intervention where binaural beat effects cannot be isolated.
6. The study is a case report, unless retained for rare clinical signal mapping.
7. The study does not report stimulation parameters with enough clarity to identify the auditory intervention.
8. The source is a review, meta-analysis, editorial, letter, or commentary. These should be marked as Background rather than included in primary extraction.

---

## 6. Background Sources

Mark as `Background` rather than `Exclude` if the source is useful for context but not eligible for primary extraction.

Examples:

- systematic reviews
- scoping reviews
- meta-analyses
- theoretical papers
- methodological papers
- historical binaural beat papers
- auditory neuroscience papers relevant to mechanism but not intervention
- sonification or auditory-motor papers not involving binaural beats

Background sources may be used for:

- introduction
- conceptual framing
- citation chasing
- operational definitions
- discussion
- translational framework

They should not be included in the empirical extraction table unless a later decision explicitly creates a separate background evidence table.

## 7. Evidence Domain Tags

Assign one or more tags to each record marked Include, Maybe, or Background.

| Tag | Domain | Description |
|---|---|---|
| MECH | Mechanism | EEG, MEG, ASSR, FFR, ERP, spectral power, connectivity, phase synchronization, entrainment |
| COG | Cognition | Attention, memory, vigilance, executive function, learning, cognitive performance |
| AFFECT | Affective state | Anxiety, mood, stress, relaxation, meditation-related outcomes |
| PAIN | Pain | Experimental pain, clinical pain, pain perception, pain modulation |
| SLEEP | Sleep | Sleep quality, insomnia, sleep onset, relaxation/sleep-adjacent outcomes |
| MOTOR | Motor control | Motor learning, motor performance, reaction time, error, dexterity, tremor, kinematics |
| NEURO | Neurological/rehabilitation | Parkinson’s disease, stroke, aging, neurological rehabilitation |
| AUTO | Autonomic/physiological | HRV, heart rate, blood pressure, arousal, autonomic regulation |
| METHODS | Methods | Protocol, measurement, stimulation design, or methodological focus |
| REVIEW | Review/background | Review article, meta-analysis, commentary, or background source |

A single record may receive multiple tags.

Example:

```text
Include | MECH; COG
Maybe | MOTOR; NEURO
Background | REVIEW; METHODS
```

## 8. Thesis Relevance Scale

Assign a thesis relevance score during full-text screening or extraction. During title/abstract screening, assign only if obvious.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not directly relevant to thesis except broad background |
| 1 | Relevant to auditory neuromodulation generally |
| 2 | Relevant to auditory-motor coupling, sensorimotor integration, timing, prediction, or movement-related auditory processing |
| 3 | Directly relevant to motor learning, motor control, kinematics, tremor, dexterity, rehabilitation, or sonification of a motor task |

---

## 9. Title/Abstract Screening Procedure

For each record:

1. Read title.
2. Read abstract.
3. Determine whether the intervention appears to involve binaural beats, binaural auditory stimulation, auditory beat stimulation, or brainwave entrainment.
4. Determine whether the record reports empirical human data.
5. Assign a screening decision:
   - Include
   - Maybe
   - Exclude
   - Background
6. Assign evidence-domain tags.
7. Record an exclusion reason if Exclude.
8. Add notes if the decision is uncertain.

### Conservative rule

If binaural beat involvement is unclear but possible, mark:

```text
Maybe
```

If the paper is clearly a review or theoretical paper, mark:

```text
Background
```

If the paper clearly does not involve binaural beats, auditory beat stimulation, or brainwave entrainment, mark:

```text
Exclude
```

---

## 10. Full-Text Screening Procedure

For each record marked Include or Maybe:

1. Retrieve full text.
2. Confirm human participants.
3. Confirm empirical data.
4. Confirm binaural beat or separable auditory beat stimulation condition.
5. Confirm at least one eligible outcome domain.
6. Check whether stimulation parameters can be extracted.
7. Assign final inclusion status:
   - Include for extraction
   - Exclude after full text
   - Background only
8. Record specific exclusion reason if excluded.
9. Assign evidence-domain tags.
10. Assign thesis relevance score.

---

## 11. Exclusion Reason Categories

Use standardized exclusion reasons.

| Code | Exclusion Reason |
|---|---|
| wrong_population | Nonhuman or not human participant research |
| wrong_intervention | No binaural beat, binaural auditory stimulation, auditory beat stimulation, or separable brainwave entrainment intervention |
| bundled_intervention | Binaural beat effect cannot be isolated from broader intervention |
| wrong_outcome | No relevant mechanistic, behavioral, clinical, motor, autonomic, or rehabilitation outcome |
| not_empirical | No empirical data |
| review_background | Review, meta-analysis, commentary, editorial, or theoretical paper retained as background |
| insufficient_parameters | Auditory stimulation parameters not sufficiently reported |
| duplicate | Duplicate record |
| unavailable_full_text | Full text unavailable after reasonable retrieval attempt |
| case_report | Case report not retained for signal mapping |
| non_peer_reviewed | Commercial, promotional, blog, or non-scholarly source |

## 13. Deduplication Rules

Deduplicate before title/abstract screening whenever possible.

Preferred duplicate matching hierarchy:

1. DOI
2. PMID or database-specific identifier
3. Exact title match
4. Highly similar title + same author + same year
5. Manual review for near-duplicates

If duplicate records differ in metadata quality, retain the most complete record.

Preferred retained record order:

1. DOI present
2. Abstract present
3. PMID present
4. Journal and volume/issue present
5. Complete author list present

Record deduplication count for PRISMA flow reporting.

## 16. Citation Chasing

Citation chasing should occur after initial screening corpus construction.

Use citation chasing for:

1. Key reviews.
2. Mechanistic binaural beat papers.
3. Motor-relevant binaural acoustic stimulation studies.
4. Parkinson’s disease, stroke, and motor-learning studies.
5. Papers repeatedly cited as foundational.

## 17. Conceptual Boundary Rule

Maintain this distinction throughout screening:

```text
Binaural beats are task-independent auditory neuromodulation.
Sonification is task-relevant auditory representation of movement or feedback.
The sine-wave visual template is a visual-motor tracking paradigm.
Future work may combine auditory neuromodulation and sonified feedback, but they should not be treated as the same mechanism.
```

This distinction is important for thesis integration and should guide tagging, notes, and later synthesis.

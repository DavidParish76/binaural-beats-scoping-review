# Operational Definitions

## Binaural Beats

A psychoacoustic percept that occurs when two tones of slightly different frequencies are presented separately to each ear, producing the perception of a third beat frequency corresponding to the frequency difference between the tones.

## Binaural Auditory Beat Stimulation

An intervention using binaural beats as an auditory stimulus, often intended to influence neural oscillations, cognition, mood, pain, sleep, or motor performance.

## Brainwave Entrainment

The hypothesis that rhythmic auditory or visual stimulation at a specific frequency can induce or synchronize neural oscillatory activity at the stimulation frequency or related harmonics.

## Monaural Beats

Physical amplitude-modulated beats created when two nearby frequencies are combined before reaching the ears, producing an actual acoustic beat rather than a purely binaural percept.

## Isochronic Tones

Regularly spaced pulses of a single tone that create a rhythmic auditory stimulus. Unlike binaural beats, isochronic tones do not require headphone-based dichotic presentation.

## Auditory Steady-State Response (ASSR)

A neural oscillatory response phase-locked to a periodic auditory stimulus, measurable with EEG or MEG. Used as an index of auditory entrainment.

## Frequency-Following Response (FFR)

An electrophysiological response generated in the auditory brainstem that mirrors the temporal structure of an auditory stimulus.

## Sonification

The transformation of data, movement, or task-relevant information into sound. In the thesis context, sonification refers to auditory representation of a sine-wave motor template or movement performance.

## Sine-Wave Visual Template

A visual-motor tracking paradigm in which participants use movement to follow or reproduce a sine-wave-like template, allowing analysis of motor accuracy, timing, prediction, and control.

## Auditory-Motor Coupling

The interaction between auditory perception and motor planning, timing, prediction, or execution.

## Motor Learning

A relatively durable change in motor performance or motor capability resulting from practice or experience.

## Motor Performance

Observable behavior during a motor task, including reaction time, movement time, error, accuracy, smoothness, tremor, variability, or kinematic parameters.

## Entrainment (Neural)

The alignment or synchronization of endogenous neural oscillations to an exogenous rhythmic stimulus. In the binaural beat context, this refers specifically to the proposed synchronization of cortical oscillations to the beat frequency.

## Carrier Frequency

The base frequency of the tone presented to each ear in binaural beat stimulation. For example, if the left ear receives 200 Hz and the right ear receives 210 Hz, the carrier frequencies are 200 Hz and 210 Hz.

## Beat Frequency

The difference between the two carrier frequencies, perceived as the rate of the binaural beat. In the example above, the beat frequency would be 10 Hz (alpha band).
